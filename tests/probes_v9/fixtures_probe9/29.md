# Three Common Mistakes Make Planche Lean Presses Ineffective

Planche lean presses become ineffective or counterproductive when the trainee does not lean far enough forward, fails to stay high above the ground during the press, or performs the movement too quickly without control.

## Why This Matters

An exercise performed with incorrect technique does not just fail to produce results—it can reinforce bad movement patterns that make the actual planche harder to achieve. Identifying and correcting these three mistakes is essential for the exercise to transfer to planche progress.

## Details

- **Insufficient lean depth**: The trainee must lean forward until the wrist is in line with the lower back. Leaning only partway reduces the load on the shoulders and does not train the correct planche lean angle.
- **Not staying high above the ground**: The trainee must press forward and upward, not just forward. Pressing forward without upward intention keeps the body low, which trains the wrong shoulder angle and does not build the protraction needed for the planche.
- **Performing the movement too fast**: Fast reps use momentum rather than muscular control, bypassing the time under tension that builds planche-specific strength. The concentric and eccentric phases must both be slow and controlled.
- Performing the exercise with any of these mistakes can actually make the trainee's planche worse by reinforcing incorrect positioning and muscle activation patterns.
- The correct execution requires conscious attention to all three elements simultaneously—lean depth, height, and tempo—which is why the exercise is challenging even for advanced trainees.

## Grounded Example

A trainee performs planche lean presses with a shallow lean, low body position, and fast tempo, completing twelve reps easily. After being coached to lean until the wrist aligns with the lower back, press upward while leaning forward, and perform each rep over a three-second count, they can only complete four reps with good form—but report that the corrected version produces the exact feeling of the full planche position.
