# Volume Normalizes Acquired Calisthenics Skills

Once a calisthenics skill has been unlocked through intensity work, volume training — more time under tension, higher reps, and shorter rests — is what normalizes it, building endurance, control, and the ability to perform multiple repetitions or combinations within the skill.

## Why This Matters

A skill that has been unlocked but not normalized remains fragile: the trainee can perform it only when fresh, with perfect conditions, and for a single brief hold. Volume training transforms the skill into a reliable, repeatable movement that can be performed under fatigue and in varied contexts.

## Details

- The volume approach accumulates more time under tension within a skill the trainee can already perform, building endurance and control.
- As the trainee progresses and unlocks the full planche, they gradually transition from pure intensity to combining both approaches, eventually working primarily with volume.
- The same principle applies to all calisthenics skills: unlocking requires maximal strength work; normalizing requires volume and variety.
- Volume does not mean random high-rep work — it means systematically increasing hold times, adding more sets, and eventually incorporating dynamic movements within the skill.

## Grounded Example

A trainee who has just unlocked a three-second full planche hold should continue intensity work to solidify the strength base. Only after the hold is reliable for multiple sets should they begin adding volume through longer holds, more sets, and eventually dynamic movements such as planche push-ups.
