# Iron Cross And Front Lever Interfere With Each Other Despite Sharing The Same Prime Movers

Iron Cross and Front Lever both use the lats as a prime mover, but the humerus action is too different—adduction in Iron Cross versus retroflexion in Front Lever—so training one immediately before the other degrades performance rather than transferring strength.

## Why This Matters

Trainees who assume that similar muscle groups mean compatible skills pair Iron Cross and Front Lever in the same workout, wasting attempts because the first skill fatigues the muscle at a joint angle that does not carry over to the second skill.

## Details

- Iron Cross uses the lats primarily for adduction (pulling the arms down toward the body from the sides), while Front Lever uses the lats for retroflexion (pulling the arms backward from overhead).
- Even though both are lat-dominant, the different angle of humerus action means strength built in one does not transfer to the other within the same session.
- Performing Iron Cross sets first and then attempting Front Lever almost guarantees a sub-maximal Front Lever performance.

## Grounded Example

A calisthenics athlete does three sets of Iron Cross holds on rings, then immediately attempts a straddle front lever hold on a bar. Despite feeling strong from the Iron Cross work, the front lever hold collapses after two seconds—two seconds shorter than his usual performance—because the lats are fatigued in the adduction pattern and cannot produce the retroflexion pattern at full strength.
