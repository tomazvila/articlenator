# Elbows Must Be Placed Under The Core During The 90-Degree Handstand Push-Up Negative

The elbows must rotate inward and press against the core at the bottom of the 90-degree handstand push-up negative; flaring the elbows outward removes the structural support that holds the body in the horizontal position and makes the movement significantly harder.

## Why This Matters

Elbow placement is the single most common technique error in the 90-degree handstand push-up. Trainees who keep the elbows wide cannot stabilize the body at the 90-degree position and either fall or cannot push back up, wasting weeks of training on a correctable cue.

## Details

- From the handstand, the elbows must be actively pulled inward during the descent so they land under the core, not outside it.
- With elbows flared outward, the shoulders bear the load in a mechanically disadvantaged position and the body has no shelf to rest on at the bottom.
- The cue is to bring the elbows as close to the core as possible — touching the ribs or abdominal wall — to create a stable fulcrum.
- Parallettes set slightly wider than shoulder width help create space for the inward elbow rotation.
- A relaxed core compounds the problem: even with correct elbow placement, a loose midsection prevents the elbows from finding a stable anchor point.

## Grounded Example

A trainee lowers from handstand with elbows staying wide at shoulder width. At the 90-degree position, the body continues dropping past horizontal because there is no elbow support, and the trainee crashes to the ground. After correcting to pull the elbows inward against the core on the next attempt, the same trainee holds the 90-degree position steadily for three seconds.
