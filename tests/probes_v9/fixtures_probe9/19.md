# Separate Skill Days Versus Alternating Within Sessions Depends On Trainee Experience Level

Two defensible positions exist on whether planche and front lever should be trained on separate days or alternated within the same session; which holds depends on the trainee's strength level and whether they are still unlocking the skill or already performing combinations.

## Why This Matters

Trainees who blindly follow either prescription without considering their current level may waste months on the wrong training structure. The optimal arrangement shifts as the athlete progresses from beginner to advanced.

## Details

- The case for separate days: For trainees who are still learning the planche or normalizing the movement, separating skills into A (planche) and B (front lever) sessions allows more focus and emphasis on one skill at a time, preventing energy from being split across too many demands. Four to five training sessions per week, with planche two to three times and front lever two times, provides sufficient frequency for each skill.
- The case for alternating within sessions (existing vault consensus): Assigning each skill its own day limits productive attempts per week to roughly six per skill, while alternating push and pull within the same session doubles productive attempts to roughly twelve and reduces nervous system fatigue.
- The crux: The existing vault's argument assumes the trainee is strong enough that alternating does not degrade quality on either skill. The separate-days argument assumes the beginner needs concentrated focus to build the strength base for each skill independently. Once the trainee can already perform planche combinations, separating skills is no longer necessary.

## Grounded Example

A beginner who can barely hold a tuck planche for three seconds and has never attempted a front lever tries alternating planche and front lever attempts within the same session. Each front lever attempt is so unfamiliar that it drains focus from the planche work, and neither skill improves. Switching to dedicated planche sessions (three times per week) and dedicated front lever sessions (two times per week) allows the trainee to build each skill's baseline strength before attempting to combine them.
