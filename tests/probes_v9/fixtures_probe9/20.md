# Technique Corrections Must Be Prioritized One At A Time To Allow Habit Formation

When a trainee has multiple technique flaws, switching focus between corrections every week prevents any single correction from becoming an automatic habit, wasting weeks of practice.

## Why This Matters

A common trap is hearing advice from multiple sources and switching focus each time a new tip appears—for example, working on core engagement for a week, then switching to head position the next week, while the original core mistake remains unfixed and prevents progress. The same principle applies to technique corrections as to training methods: sustained focus on one change at a time produces results.

## Details

- A common pattern is hearing one tip about core engagement, working on it for a week, then hearing about head position and switching focus—while the original core mistake remains unfixed.
- If the core is relaxed and the head position is also imperfect, fix the core first because it is the more fundamental mistake, and do not switch to fixing the head until the core habit is established.
- Each technique correction requires weeks of consistent conscious self-monitoring to replace the old motor pattern; switching before the habit is locked in resets the process.

## Grounded Example
A trainee works on straddle planche leans with a sagging lower back and a forward-tilted head. In week one the focus is posterior pelvic tilt; in week two a forum post redirects the focus to head position, and in week three back to the hips. After a month neither flaw improves, because each correction restarts before the last one sticks. The trainee then commits to pelvic tilt alone for four weeks, cueing it on every lean without touching the head issue. By week six the tilt happens without conscious thought, holds gain three seconds, and the head position issue is addressed in the next block from a stable base.
