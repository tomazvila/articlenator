# A Four-Week Advanced Tuck To Straddle Planche Progression Uses Frog Straddle Pike Straddle And Half Pike As Weekly Benchmarks

Starting from a clean five-to-eight-second advanced tuck planche, a trainee can reach the straddle planche in four weeks by following a weekly progression through frog straddle, pike straddle, half-pike straddle, and the final straddle lift, with hold-time benchmarks at each step.

## Why This Matters

Most planche programs present the ladder as a single monotonic climb, but the jump from advanced tuck to straddle contains four distinct mechanical steps that each require dedicated practice. A weekly progression with specific hold targets prevents trainees from wasting time on the wrong drill and keeps every session productive.

## Details

- **Week one — Frog straddle (frog stand with tricep legs):** Start from frog stand with knees on the elbows, then open the legs to the sides with knees still bent, push down hard through the arms, and hold for eight to ten seconds. The knees must stay on the elbows and the legs must be actively opened.
- **Week two — Pike straddle (locked knees, squeezed legs):** From the frog straddle, lock the knees straight, squeeze the thighs, glutes, and calves, and point the toes. The legs remain bent at the hips (in a pike angle) — the goal is not the horizontal line yet but the engaged, locked-leg position. Hold for eight to ten seconds.
- **Week three — Half-pike straddle:** From the pike straddle, open the legs wider, squeeze the glutes and core, and lean slightly forward to raise the hips. The legs rise from the pike angle toward the horizontal line but stop halfway. Hold for eight to ten seconds.
- **Week four — Straddle planche:** From the half-pike, squeeze the lower back (after engaging core and glutes first) and lean forward and upward to lift the legs to the horizontal straddle line. The goal is a three-second straddle planche hold.
- The system assumes four planche training sessions per week.
- The progression is self-correcting: if the trainee cannot hit the eight-to-ten-second hold target for a given week, they stay at that step until the target is reached before advancing.
- The hardest transition is week three to week four (half-pike to straddle), where the lower back must be activated in the correct sequence.

## Grounded Example

A trainee with a seven-second clean advanced tuck planche starts the four-week progression. Week one: frog straddle — opens legs from frog stand, pushes down, holds for six seconds on the first session and nine seconds by the fourth session. Week two: pike straddle — locks knees, squeezes everything, holds for eight seconds by session four. Week three: half-pike — opens wider, leans forward, holds for seven seconds. Week four: from half-pike, squeezes lower back after core and glutes, leans forward and up, and achieves a three-second straddle planche hold by the end of the week.
