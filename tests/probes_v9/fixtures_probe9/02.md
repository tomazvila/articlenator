# MOC Isometric Training

Entry point for notes on isometric calisthenics training principles, intensity zones, and volume management for advanced static holds.

## Framework & Intensity
- [[Isometric Holds In Calisthenics Map To The One-Rep Max Framework]] — two seconds of isometric hold at maximum effort equals one repetition, enabling percentage-based training zones for static skills
- [[Isometric Skill Progress Requires Training In The 80 To 90 Percent One-Rep Max Zone]] — consistent progress requires 8-16 second holds (80-90% of 1RM) rather than maxing out at 2-3 second holds
- [[Intensity Is Defined As Percentage Of One-Rep Max Not Perceived Effort]] — the %1RM definition that makes the isometric-to-weightlifting mapping possible
- [[Dynamic And Isometric Strength Training Develop Different Force Production Patterns And Are Not Interchangeable]] — isometric force production is distinct from dynamic, justifying a separate training framework

## Volume Management
- [[Isometric Skill Training Volume Should Be Capped At 30 Seconds Per Session]] — total hold volume per session should not exceed 30-40 seconds or 20-30 total reps to avoid connective tissue irritation and nervous system fatigue
- [[Volume And Intensity Have An Inverse Relationship In Calisthenics]] — why high volume at high intensity is unsustainable for isometric work
- [[Training To Failure Every Session Causes Nervous System Fatigue And Progressive Regression]] — the consequence of exceeding the volume cap by training at maximal intensity every session
- [[Skill Work Sets Should Keep One Rep In Reserve To Maintain Quality]] — the RIR principle that enforces the volume cap by preventing fatigue-driven form breakdown
- [[Progressive Overload For Isometric Skills Is Applied By Working Harder Per Set Not By Increasing Volume]] — the overload method that operates within the fixed volume cap
