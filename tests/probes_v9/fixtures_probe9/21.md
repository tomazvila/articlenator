# There Is No Magic Rep Range Barrier Between Strength And Hypertrophy

Strength and hypertrophy are not separated by fixed rep ranges; low-rep recommendations for strength exist because heavy loads demand neural adaptation and tendon stiffness, not because hypertrophy stops above five reps.

## Why This Matters

Trainees who believe in a hard boundary between "strength reps" and "size reps" design needlessly rigid programs and miss that the same set can serve either goal depending on how it is executed.

## Details

- The common split of one-to-five reps for strength and eight-to-twelve for size has no physiological boundary.
- Heavy loads require coordination, high recruitment, and stiff tendons, which is why strength work clusters at low reps.
- Effective sets, not rep ranges, determine the adaptation.
- A trainee can get stronger in higher rep ranges as long as the sets are hard enough.

## Grounded Example

A trainee spends a block doing sets of twelve to fifteen on weighted dips taken close to failure. His dip one-rep max rises even though he never trained below eight reps, because the added muscle and recruitment transferred to maximal force.
